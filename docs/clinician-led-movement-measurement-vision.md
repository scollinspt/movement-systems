# Clinician-Led Movement-System Measurement Research Vision

## Status

**Status:** Proposed concept note  
**Purpose:** Preserve and refine the scientific rationale connecting Collins's prior
work in physiological dynamics to the HMS Lab's movement-system measurement program  
**Maturity:** Research vision, not a capability or clinical-validity claim

This note may provide the foundation for a future white paper. It should be revised with
formal scholarship, verified citations, colleague review, and evidence from actual method
development before being represented as a settled program statement.

## Central Proposition

Human systems should be studied through their changing patterns of coordination,
variability, and adaptation in context. Field-capable longitudinal measurement can
extend this systems approach to movement behavior, enabling clinician-originated
questions that laboratory snapshots and static summaries cannot adequately address.

The objective is not measurement for its own sake. The program seeks to develop a
clinician-led measurement science through which clinicians can better understand the
movement system and design, evaluate, and refine efforts to improve it.

## Intellectual Continuity

The program deliberately continues a line of inquiry from Collins's dissertation work
on job strain and heart-rate variability. That work examined physiological regulation
through dynamic variability and autonomic coordination, considered context alongside
physiological observations, and identified the limits of static summaries and short
observation windows.

The HMS Lab extends the underlying complex-systems framework from autonomic coordination
to movement-system coordination:

```text
context and psychosocial conditions
              |
              v
autonomic regulation, variability, and coordination
              |
              v
adaptation and possible loss of regulatory control
              |
              v
movement-system organization across person, task, and environment
              |
              v
field-capable and longitudinal observation
              |
              v
clinician-originated questions and attempts to improve movement
```

The dissertation does not itself establish movement-system applications, long-term
movement logging, clinical utility, or clinician-led implementation. Those are intended
extensions that require their own conceptual, methodological, and empirical development.

## Problem To Be Addressed

Much movement research is constrained by brief laboratory observations, equipment-bound
tasks, static summaries, narrow disciplinary questions, and limited connection to
clinical practice. Clinicians may contribute access, participants, or interpretation
without equivalent authority over question formation, construct definition, method
selection, or warranted claims.

That arrangement can leave important questions unasked:

- Does a movement strategy persist outside the clinic or laboratory?
- How does organization change across tasks, environments, symptoms, fatigue, and time?
- When does variability indicate adaptability, and when does it indicate restricted
  control or loss of stability?
- Does an observed intervention response generalize to ordinary activity?
- What transitions precede functional improvement, recurrence, or decline?
- Which clinically meaningful properties can be recovered from sparse and accessible
  measurements?

## Scientific Commitments

### Movement As An Emergent Systems Phenomenon

Movement behavior emerges from coupled physiological, neural, muscular, mechanical,
behavioral, task, and environmental processes. No single signal, segment, model, or
discipline is the movement system.

### Dynamics Before Static Description

Means, endpoints, and isolated snapshots may obscure coordination, fluctuation,
adaptation, recovery, transition, and context dependence. The program should preserve
within-person dynamics when the question requires them.

### Multiple Timescales

Relevant behavior may unfold within repetitions, sessions, days, episodes of care, and
longer periods of maintenance or recurrence. Sampling and analysis should match the
timescale of the claim.

### Field Measurement As A Scientific Capability

Portability is not merely a lower-cost substitute for laboratory instrumentation.
Bringing measurement to movement can reveal behavior under authentic tasks,
environments, constraints, and durations that cannot be reproduced adequately in a
single laboratory session.

### Clinical Meaning Requires Context

Movement signals should be interpreted with patient goals, symptoms, function, task,
environment, treatment, exertion, and clinician observations when those factors are
relevant. A technically valid signal does not acquire clinical meaning automatically.

### Questions Before Instruments

Clinically consequential questions and explicit constructs should determine what is
measured. Available sensors must not silently define the scientific agenda.

## Persistence, Variability, And Adaptation

Persistence should not initially be reduced to one metric. Depending on the question, it
may refer to:

- recurrence of a movement strategy;
- stability or change across repetitions and sessions;
- retention after an intervention;
- generalization across tasks or environments;
- recovery after perturbation, symptoms, fatigue, or interruption;
- maintenance of function over longer periods; or
- transitions among distinguishable movement regimes.

Variability is likewise not inherently healthy or pathological. Interpretation depends
on structure, scale, task, environment, goals, constraints, and consequences. Research
should distinguish adaptable variation from noise, measurement error, instability, or
restricted control rather than assuming that more or less variability is universally
desirable.

## Clinician Leadership

Clinician leadership means more than usability testing or post hoc interpretation.
Clinicians should participate in:

- recognizing consequential phenomena;
- originating and prioritizing questions;
- defining constructs and clinically meaningful contexts;
- identifying patient-important consequences;
- selecting acceptable burdens and risks;
- interpreting observations alongside the examination and patient experience;
- determining what evidence would change practice; and
- limiting claims that exceed the method or context.

Engineers, quantitative scientists, and other collaborators remain essential. Their role
is to make questions measurable and methods trustworthy without displacing the clinical
meaning that motivated the work.

## Educational Role

Transformative clinicians do not merely consume evidence or operate technology. They can
recognize what current measures omit, formulate testable questions, collaborate across
disciplines, interpret dynamic evidence in context, and evaluate whether attempts to
improve movement persist and generalize.

Every DPT student should develop a foundation for participating in this measurement
science. A smaller group may pursue deeper method development and research through
optional curricular and extracurricular HMS Lab activities. The detailed pathway is
defined in the [Course–Lab Integration Framework](course-lab-integration.md).

## Role Of Movesense–OpenSense

Movesense, OpenSense, OpenSim, and related tools are enabling components, not the research
identity. The current program can establish:

- transparent access to raw and derived observations;
- known timing, synchronization, calibration, and orientation behavior;
- portable multi-sensor acquisition;
- reproducible transformations into biomechanical workflows; and
- explicit uncertainty and failure boundaries.

Technical interoperability establishes neither movement-system validity nor clinical
utility. The measurement stack should mature through staged non-human, governed human,
method-validation, field, and longitudinal work tied to specific questions and claims.

## Research Program

The emerging program includes at least four connected lines of work:

1. **Measurement foundations:** timing, calibration, orientation, synchronization,
   observability, uncertainty, and reproducibility.
2. **Movement-system constructs:** coordination, variability, stability, adaptation,
   persistence, transition, and person–task–environment organization.
3. **Field and longitudinal methods:** context-aware repeated measurement across the
   timescales required by clinical questions.
4. **Clinical and educational translation:** clinician-originated questions, student
   development, exploratory clinical adjuncts, method-specific evidence, and eventual
   evidence-qualified use.

Each line should retain distinct maturity and governance. Progress in one does not imply
readiness in the others.

## Claims This Vision Does Not Yet Support

The program does not yet establish that:

- the HMS Lab provides an operational clinical measurement service;
- Movesense-derived measures are valid for diagnosis or treatment decisions;
- movement persistence or adaptability has one accepted operational definition;
- field measurement is superior to laboratory measurement for every question;
- longitudinal data alone establish mechanism or causation;
- patient or student activities are research; or
- any specific measurement improves outcomes.

## Toward A White Paper

A future white paper could develop:

1. the clinical and scientific problem;
2. the intellectual lineage from physiological dynamics to movement-system dynamics;
3. the complex adaptive systems framework;
4. limitations of static, laboratory-bound, and engineer-led paradigms;
5. requirements for field-capable longitudinal measurement;
6. a clinician-led research and governance model;
7. the educational pathway for transformative clinicians;
8. a staged technical and clinical research agenda; and
9. falsifiable propositions, competing explanations, and limits.

Before drafting that paper, the program should collect perspectives from students and
supervising clinicians, refine its key constructs, identify representative clinical
questions, and gain practical experience with the initial measurement stack.
