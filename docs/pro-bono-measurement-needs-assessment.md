# Pro Bono Clinic Movement-Measurement Needs Assessment

## Status And Purpose

**Status:** Draft for internal planning review
**Intended respondents:** DPT students and supervising clinicians/faculty involved in
the Pro Bono Clinic
**Initial use:** Internal educational, laboratory, and service-development planning
**Estimated completion time:** 10–15 minutes online or 20–30 minutes as an interview

This shared questionnaire explores whether closer Pro Bono Clinic–HMS Lab integration is
worth pursuing, which needs it might address, what alternatives should be considered,
and what safeguards would be required. It can be administered as an anonymous online
form or used as a semi-structured interview guide.

This is not a patient questionnaire, clinical intake form, research protocol, or claim
that the HMS Lab currently provides a clinical measurement service. Do not enter names,
dates of birth, diagnoses, appointment dates, unusual case details, or other information
that could identify a patient, student, or respondent.

Before administration, the responsible faculty member should confirm applicable
institutional requirements and document the data custodian, access, retention period,
and destruction date. Responses collected for internal planning are not research data
and must not be repurposed as research without the required institutional determination
and a prospective plan.

No research reuse is assumed or authorized. Method-specific evidence is required before
a measurement may directly inform care.

## Administration Safeguards

For an anonymous online form:

- do not collect names, email addresses, account identifiers, or IP addresses where the
  platform permits that setting;
- do not require a response to the optional role question or any free-text question;
- limit free-text responses to two or three sentences;
- report role-specific results only when at least five people are in that group; and
- do not publish or quote individual responses.

For facilitated interviews:

- use the same questions and order for every respondent;
- schedule 20–30 minutes;
- take structured notes under the question headings rather than recording by default;
- use only the neutral probes listed below;
- omit details that could identify a respondent or patient; and
- combine notes into themes before sharing a summary.

Participation should be voluntary. Choosing not to respond, disagreeing with the
proposal, or declining future HMS Lab involvement should have no effect on a student's
course or clinic standing or a clinician's role.

## Suggested Introduction For Respondents

The Human Movement Systems Laboratory (HMS Lab) is being developed to support
clinician-led questions about movement through accessible, portable, and potentially
longitudinal measurement. This assessment asks whether a Pro Bono Clinic–HMS Lab patient-measurement pathway should be developed and, if so, what form it should take.

One possibility are optional educational and exploratory HMS Lab measurement sessions or even a semester during one of a student's final two Pro Bono Clinic semesters. Other options
include non-patient classroom activities, case discussions, synthetic-data exercises,
or a formal clinic–lab pathway. Exploratory measurements could contribute an additional
observation or hypothesis. With time PSU DPT may develop and research specific methods that could inform care more directly after sufficient evidence and operational readiness are established for its intended use.

Please answer from your own educational or supervisory perspective. Do not describe an
identifiable patient or include protected health information.

## Neutral Interview Probes

Use a probe only when clarification is needed, and apply it consistently across
respondents:

- What makes that important?
- What alternative would you prefer?
- What information would change your view?
- What could make that measurement misleading?
- What would a useful outcome look like?
- What evidence would you need before using a method to inform care more directly?

If a respondent begins describing an identifiable patient, interrupt respectfully and
ask for the general category of question instead. Do not retain the identifying detail
in notes.

## Suggested Internal Planning Analysis

The assessment informs deliberation; it is not a referendum and has no automatic
threshold that authorizes a pilot or service.

- Report counts and distributions for scaled and multiple-choice responses.
- Use medians and response distributions rather than treating ordinal scales as precise
  interval measurements.
- Summarize recurring free-text themes without individual quotations.
- Compare students and supervisors only when each reported group contains at least five
  respondents; otherwise report combined results.
- Identify support, opposition, uncertainty, alternatives, barriers, and conditions for
  reconsideration.
- Preserve meaningful disagreement rather than converting it into a single average.
- Record decisions supported, questions still open, and issues requiring institutional
  review.

Do not place raw responses, interview notes, or potentially identifying subgroup results
in Git or on the public website. Any use beyond internal planning requires a new review
of purpose, governance, consent, privacy, and data handling; existing responses must not
be assumed available for research or publication.
