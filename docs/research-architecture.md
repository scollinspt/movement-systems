# Research Architecture

## Purpose

Movement Systems is the public scholarly home for theory, concepts, methods, and
research about human movement as an emergent systems phenomenon. The Human Movement
Systems Laboratory (HMS Lab) is the empirical research program that observes and
measures movement, tests explanations, and connects experimental findings to
mechanistic and computational models.

The laboratory and the public knowledge project are related but not identical:

- **HMS Lab** organizes people, measurement, experiments, governance, and collaboration.
- **Movement Systems** organizes the resulting questions, concepts, methods, public
  records, interpretations, educational translations, and website.

Neither is defined primarily by a course, a particular instrument, or a simulation
platform.

## Scientific Position

Human movement is treated as behavior emerging from coupled physiological, neural,
muscular, mechanical, behavioral, task, and environmental systems. The program is
mechanistic and generative: it asks what interacting mechanisms are sufficient to
produce an observed phenomenon, not only how to describe or predict that phenomenon.

The core inquiry cycle is:

```text
observe a phenomenon
        |
        v
formulate questions and competing mechanisms
        |
        v
select measurements and characterize uncertainty
        |
        v
collect and analyze observations
        |
        v
construct or revise mechanistic models
        |
        v
generate behavior and compare it with observation
        |
        +---------------------> revise the question, method, or model
```

One foundational methodological question is how much scientifically important movement
structure can be recovered from strategically chosen, accessible measurements. This
supports work in observability, dimensionality, state estimation, sensor fusion,
measurement uncertainty, system identification, movement variability, nonlinear
dynamics, and model identifiability.

## Research Domains

The program may develop work across several connected domains:

- movement systems theory and conceptual synthesis;
- rehabilitation and functional movement;
- motor control, motor learning, and adaptation;
- biomechanics, neuromechanics, and neuromuscular function;
- portable and multimodal movement measurement;
- physiological-movement integration and fatigue;
- ergonomics, occupational movement, and human performance;
- human-device interaction and assistive technology;
- observability, uncertainty, sensor fusion, and methodological validation; and
- mechanistic, dynamical, and neuromusculoskeletal modeling.

These are organizing domains, not claims that every capability is currently operational.

## Clinical, Laboratory, And Educational Cycle

The Pro Bono Clinic, DPT program, HMS Lab, and computational research program form a
potential inquiry cycle:

```text
clinical or educational observation
              |
              v
question formation and literature inquiry
              |
              v
HMS Lab measurement or experiment
              |
              v
evidence, mechanism, and model development
              |
              v
reviewed knowledge and educational translation
              |
              v
new clinical, educational, or research questions
```

Movement from a clinical question to research inquiry should be intellectually easy.
Movement from a patient to a research participant must be deliberate and governed.
Clinical care, education, research, and diagnostic services remain distinct activities
with separate requirements for consent, privacy, oversight, documentation, and claims.

Courses are a translation and question-generating environment. They are not the system
of record for research findings, and classroom observations do not become research data
or accepted claims merely because they are educationally useful.

## Clinical Inquiry Ecosystem

The HMS Lab adds an empirical movement component to the Clinical Inquiry Ecosystem:

- **HMS Lab** develops observations, measurements, experiments, and movement questions.
- **stats4PT** examines what observations can support as evidence and warranted claims.
- **Physiolog** develops authoritative physiological explanations and generative
  mechanisms.
- **Movement Systems** develops movement-domain theory, mechanisms, methods, and public
  synthesis.
- **movement-models-** implements specialized ontology work, model mappings,
  computational experiments, and reproducible technical artifacts.
- **Models4PT** curates evidence and mechanisms into population causal knowledge.
- **Clinical Inference Engine** applies versioned population knowledge in inspectable,
  patient-specific reasoning.
- **scollinspt** explains the unified professional program and provides discovery.

Transfers among these projects are deliberate, reviewed, and versioned. No repository
silently mutates another project's authoritative content.

## Repository Authority

This repository is authoritative for:

- public movement-systems theory and definitions;
- the HMS Lab's public research agenda and program descriptions;
- public research questions and project records;
- public methods, protocols, and capability statements;
- non-identifiable, permission-cleared results and research outputs;
- public interpretations of movement models and simulations;
- educational translations approved for broad distribution; and
- the source and governance of `movementsystems.org`.

This repository is not authoritative for:

- semester course operations or restricted teaching materials;
- identifiable participant, patient, or student records;
- raw or controlled research data;
- canonical Physiolog explanations;
- specialized model execution owned by `movement-models-`;
- Models4PT population causal knowledge; or
- patient-specific conclusions from the Clinical Inference Engine.

## Research Lifecycle

Public research records should progress through explicit states rather than implying
more maturity than the work supports:

1. **Proposed**: a question, hypothesis, capability, or method has been articulated.
2. **In development**: design, inventory, protocol, implementation, or validation is
   underway.
3. **Operational**: a method or capability has passed its stated readiness checks and
   can be used within its documented limits.
4. **Analyzed**: observations have been analyzed under a documented method, but claims
   may still be under review.
5. **Established result**: a specific result has completed its stated scientific review
   or publication process.
6. **Superseded**: the record remains available for provenance but is no longer the
   recommended account.

Status applies to a specific claim. An operational sensor does not validate every
derived measure; a model that executes does not establish physiological or clinical
validity; and one established result does not validate an entire laboratory capability.

## Program Development Order

Near-term development should follow this order:

1. inventory actual equipment, software, licenses, spaces, and data access;
2. define representative scientific and translational use cases;
3. specify measurement, synchronization, calibration, computing, and governance needs;
4. identify repeated capability gaps before purchasing equipment;
5. develop a small portfolio of controlled, portable, and computational pilot projects;
6. publish capabilities only after their readiness and limits are documented; and
7. develop collaboration and fundraising materials from authentic work rather than
   projected capability.

## Public Communication Rules

- Distinguish vision, planned work, current capability, and established result.
- Describe verified tasks and measures rather than listing equipment as proof of ability.
- State uncertainty, validation scope, assumptions, and important negative findings.
- Do not imply current company partnerships where none exist.
- Do not imply that the HMS Lab currently provides clinical diagnostic services.
- Do not imply that clinic patients or students are automatically research participants.
- Keep scientific language accessible without replacing precision with promotion.
