# Movement Systems Course–HMS Lab Integration Framework

## Status

**Status:** Proposed planning framework  
**Scope:** Public, durable relationship among the DPT Movement Systems course, the HMS
Lab, the Pro Bono Clinic, and the broader movement-system research program  
**Not yet approved:** Any patient-facing HMS Lab service, required student laboratory
participation, or use of clinical or educational activity as research

Operational course documents remain in the private PTH6132 workspace and Canvas. Raw
questionnaire responses, identifiable student or patient information, clinical records,
and governed research materials do not belong in this repository.

## Purpose

The Movement Systems course and HMS Lab should form a deliberate educational and
scientific relationship without becoming the same activity. The course introduces every
DPT student to the identity, concepts, and reasoning practices needed to participate in
movement-system measurement science. Optional curricular and extracurricular HMS Lab
activities provide repeated opportunities for interested students to deepen, apply, and
extend that capacity.

The ultimate purpose is clinical. Education develops clinicians capable of participating
in measurement science so they can better understand the movement system and design,
evaluate, and refine efforts to improve it.

## Shared Proposition

Human movement is an emergent behavior of interacting physiological, neural, muscular,
mechanical, behavioral, task, and environmental systems. Understanding and improving
movement therefore requires more than isolated observations, static summaries, or
instrument-defined questions. Clinicians should be able to originate consequential
questions, help define what should be measured, interpret observations in context, and
evaluate whether change persists and generalizes.

The relationship is reciprocal:

```text
clinical and educational observation
              |
              v
question formation and operationalization
              |
              v
HMS Lab measurement, method development, or experiment
              |
              v
contextual interpretation and warranted claims
              |
              v
education, clinical reasoning, and attempts to improve movement
              |
              +--------------------> new observations and questions
```

## Distinct Roles

### Movement Systems Course

The course establishes the shared professional foundation. Every student should learn
to:

- recognize movement as behavior emerging from a complex adaptive system;
- translate clinical observations into meaningful, testable questions;
- distinguish a construct from an instrument, signal, metric, or model;
- reason about coordination, variability, adaptation, context, and timescale;
- evaluate validity, reliability, responsiveness, uncertainty, and ecological relevance;
- interpret repeated and longitudinal observations without overclaiming causation;
- collaborate with engineers and scientists without surrendering clinical meaning;
- recognize governance, privacy, consent, and data-stewardship responsibilities; and
- use measurement evidence to evaluate and refine attempts to improve movement.

The course is not an engineering course and should not require every student to become a
sensor operator or data analyst.

### HMS Lab

The HMS Lab provides an empirical environment in which clinically meaningful questions
can be operationalized, methods can be developed and challenged, and interested students
can gain deeper experience. It should:

- begin with the question rather than the available equipment;
- make assumptions, uncertainty, and unsupported interpretations visible;
- preserve the distinction among education, clinical care, service development, and
  research;
- provide progressive participation appropriate to method and student readiness;
- return interpretable observations rather than technology-centered output; and
- create routes from unanswered clinical questions to governed research.

### Pro Bono Clinic

The Pro Bono Clinic is a potential source of clinical questions and a future setting for
appropriately governed educational measurement. It is not automatically a recruitment
pool or research environment. Patient care, voluntary educational participation, and
research participation remain separate.

### Movesense–OpenSense Program

The Movesense–OpenSense program develops one enabling measurement stack. It does not
define the scientific agenda or establish clinical utility merely by functioning.
Methods mature claim by claim and use case by use case.

## Student Development Pathway

### 1. Foundational Participation

Required Movement Systems course experiences introduce all students to the identity and
reasoning framework of measurement science. Learning emphasizes observation, question
formation, operationalization, interpretation, and limits rather than platform mastery.

### 2. Applied Development

Optional curricular or extracurricular activities may include:

- Movement Inquiry Meetings or case discussions;
- observation of approved HMS Lab activities;
- operationalization of de-identified or hypothetical clinical questions;
- interpretation of synthetic, public, or appropriately governed data;
- supervised assistance with an approved measurement session; and
- method-development and quality-control exercises.

### 3. Advanced Contribution

A smaller group may pursue independent study, capstone work, research practica, or
longer-term projects involving:

- acquisition, calibration, synchronization, and protocol development;
- time-series and movement-data analysis;
- reproducible data pipelines and quality control;
- method validation and study design;
- clinician-originated research; and
- scholarly interpretation and dissemination.

Participation at one level does not automatically authorize activity at another.

## Reasoning Progression

The shared developmental sequence is:

```text
observe -> question -> operationalize -> measure -> interpret -> improve -> reassess
```

The sequence is iterative. A failed measurement may revise the operational definition;
an unexpected observation may revise the question; and a change observed in the lab may
require field or longitudinal observation before persistence or generalization can be
claimed.

## Proposed Pro Bono Clinic–HMS Lab Pathway

One possible future option would allow interested treating students to request that one
of a patient's final two Pro Bono Clinic sessions occur in the HMS Lab. The pathway is a
planning concept, not an approved or operational service.

An initial workflow could be:

1. Treating students identify a specific movement-related clinical question.
2. The supervising clinician determines whether an exploratory measurement session is
   appropriate and within the limits of available methods.
3. The patient receives an accessible explanation and may agree or decline without any
   effect on usual care.
4. Students and supervisors translate the question into constructs, observables, and a
   bounded measurement plan.
5. The lab collects only the information needed for that question under an approved
   educational or clinical workflow.
6. The team interprets findings with patient goals, symptoms, function, context, and
   ordinary examination findings.
7. The team records whether the session added useful understanding and what uncertainty
   remains.

Initially, such sessions would be educational and exploratory adjuncts to care. Findings
may contribute an additional observation or clinical hypothesis, but they would not
independently determine the plan of care or be represented as validated clinical
conclusions.

## Clinical Translation Ladder

### 1. Technical Development

Non-human and appropriately governed feasibility work establishes that a measurement can
be acquired, processed, and interpreted within documented limits.

### 2. Educational And Exploratory Adjunct

An approved optional session supports learning and clinical question formation. Results
may enrich discussion or generate hypotheses but do not function as independently
validated clinical conclusions.

### 3. Evidence-Qualified Clinical Adjunct

A specific method may contribute directly to the plan of care after sufficient evidence
and operational readiness are established for the intended question, population, task,
environment, and interpretation.

### 4. Routine Clinical Integration

A mature method may become a regular component of care only with defined indications,
protocols, interpretation standards, supervision, documentation, quality assurance, and
reassessment.

Readiness belongs to a specific method and use claim, not to the HMS Lab, an instrument,
or a software pipeline as a whole. Relevant evidence may include technical and analytic
validity, repeatability, measurement uncertainty, ecological relevance, responsiveness,
incremental value, patient burden, reproducibility, and known harms or failure modes.

## Governance And Data Boundaries

- No patient, student, or supervisor becomes a research participant by taking part in
  clinical care, education, or internal planning.
- Internal needs-assessment responses are not research data and must not be repurposed
  without the required institutional determination and prospective plan.
- Patient care and optional educational measurement require appropriate supervision,
  privacy, documentation, safety, accessibility, and voluntary-participation procedures.
- Research requires its own institutional determination, protocol, consent process, data
  plan, and claim boundaries.
- Exploratory output must be labeled and communicated in language that does not imply
  diagnostic or clinical validity.
- Identifiable or controlled information remains in approved systems outside this
  repository.

## Current Planning Instrument

The [Pro Bono Clinic Movement-Measurement Needs Assessment](pro-bono-measurement-needs-assessment.md)
is a draft shared questionnaire for DPT students and supervising clinicians/faculty. It
is intended for anonymous online administration or facilitated interviews for internal
planning. It does not collect patient information and is not approved for research use.

## Open Decisions

- Which clinical questions recur often enough to justify method development?
- What forms of optional curricular and extracurricular participation are feasible?
- Which institutional offices must review an initial educational and exploratory
  Pro Bono Clinic–HMS Lab pathway?
- What method-specific evidence thresholds should govern movement up the clinical
  translation ladder?
- How should exploratory findings be documented and communicated to patients and
  treating teams?
- What evaluation would show that an HMS Lab session improves student reasoning rather
  than merely increasing exposure to technology?

The needs assessment should inform these decisions before an operational workflow is
designed.
