## Project question and rationale

Can a cost-effective maximal inspiratory pressure measurement system provide reliable
and valid measurements of the pressures generated by the ventilatory pump?

Maximal inspiratory pressure provides information about the ability of the inspiratory
muscles and ventilatory pump to generate pressure. The project renews work begun by
Collins and Cahalin approximately two decades ago on accessible measurement of
inspiratory muscle performance.

## Initial study

The initial study will develop and evaluate an accessible maximal inspiratory pressure
measurement system. Controlled pressure generation will support comparison across the
developed system and BIOPAC pressure measurement.

The planned evaluation includes:

1. defining the measurement range, interfaces, calibration procedures, and acquisition
   workflow;
2. generating pressures across a representative range;
3. evaluating agreement with BIOPAC measurements;
4. estimating intra-rater and inter-rater reliability; and
5. documenting sources of measurement variation and practical limits.

## Instrumentation

The developing measurement pathway includes:

- a BIOPAC MP160 system;
- a BIOPAC TSD160E differential-pressure transducer;
- an AFTCAL-160 differential-pressure manometer; and
- an accessible maximal inspiratory pressure measurement assembly.

## Developing directions

Later studies may extend the project through:

- synchronized pressure and chest-wall motion measurement;
- BIOPAC and wearable IMU measurement of thoracic movement;
- analysis of regional chest-wall mechanics;
- relationships among pressure generation, muscle function, and chest-wall motion; and
- computational thoracic modeling informed by the OpenSim spine and ribcage project.

## Planned outputs

- a documented maximal inspiratory pressure measurement system;
- calibration and acquisition procedures;
- validity and reliability analyses;
- a methods or short research report; and
- a foundation for subsequent ventilatory pump and chest-wall mechanics studies.

## Current status and next step

The project is in planning and instrumentation development. The next step is to confirm
the pressure-generation and comparison setup, complete the equipment configuration, and
write the initial study protocol.
