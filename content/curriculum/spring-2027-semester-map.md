## Planning Basis

The full Spring 2027 semester begins Tuesday, January 26, and ends Friday, May 14.
Spring Break is March 22-26, classes resume March 29, and finals are May 10-14.
This produces 14 instructional weeks plus finals week.

The working delivery pattern is based on the recent syllabi:

- Movement Systems meets for two lecture periods each week.
- EP&N meets for one lecture period each week.
- One shared weekly laboratory experience may be repeated across cohort sections.
- The same DPT cohort is enrolled in both courses.

Exact Spring 2027 meeting days, times, lab sections, and final-exam periods remain to be
confirmed. Dates below use the legacy Tuesday/Thursday Movement Systems and Friday EP&N
pattern only as a planning scaffold.

## Design Commitments

- Movement Systems is 4 credits and includes the shared laboratory; EP&N is 3 credits.
- Sean Collins opens Movement Systems on January 26.
- Eydie Kendall teaches normal gait on January 28, February 2, and February 4 and leads
  the normal-gait laboratory during the second week.
- Movement Systems proper begins February 9. A later atypical-gait module covers the
  displaced neuromuscular, musculoskeletal, variation, dysfunction, and clinical
  synthesis material after students have the systems concepts needed to interpret it.
- EP&N begins with the physiology of digestion, absorption, and metabolism (DAM),
  supported by the planned Chapter 13 of _Clinical Physiology: A Movement-Centered
  Approach_.
- Nutrition physiology follows DAM. Physiological adaptation to exercise follows
  nutrition physiology and will be supported by the planned Part IV of the book.
- After the gait laboratory, EP&N provides the primary content sequence for the
  shared lab while Movement Systems supplies the systems analysis and movement context.
- The same laboratory schedule will appear in both course syllabi.

## Recommended Assessment Rhythm

The two courses should retain separate grades while sharing selected evidence and cases.
A reasonable starting rhythm is:

1. **Week 3:** gait analysis checkpoint and DAM knowledge check.
2. **Week 5:** baseline measurement portfolio and nutrition-physiology checkpoint.
3. **Week 8:** pre-break synthesis of systems concepts, exercise response, and early
   adaptation.
4. **Weeks 9-13:** longitudinal prescription and movement-intervention revisions using
   the same case or client data.
5. **Week 14/finals:** Movement Systems case synthesis and EP&N client guidance,
   documentation, and reflection.

Shared lab work may supply evidence to assignments in both courses, but each syllabus
must state what is assessed in that course and avoid double-counting one performance as
two indistinguishable grades.

## Evidence and Departures from Prior Syllabi

- The Fall 2026 Dynamic Systems I syllabus establishes the prerequisite systems and
  adaptation frame but does not yet enumerate its later gait sequence. Eydie Kendall
  approved a Spring 2027 handoff of three normal-gait lectures and one lab, conditional
  on later coverage of displaced material. The Week 10 atypical-gait module provides
  that coverage within Movement Systems.
- The Spring 2026 EP&N syllabus moves from screening and baseline testing through DAM,
  FITT prescription, muscular and cardiorespiratory fitness, progression, PRE/SAID,
  performance, lifestyle, recovery, retesting, and final client guidance. This draft
  retains that applied arc but moves DAM and nutrition physiology to the front and makes
  physiological adaptation the bridge into prescription.
- The Spring 2025 Movement Systems syllabus names foundational motor-learning concepts
  followed by functional movement, balance and postural control, sit-to-stand,
  locomotion, and reach/grasp/object manipulation. This draft retains those application
  areas but places explicit systems concepts before them.
- The PTH6111 Clinical Physiology source establishes the book-based instructional pattern
  and the existing physiology foundation. Chapter 13 DAM and Part IV adaptation remain
  development dependencies, not completed assigned readings.
